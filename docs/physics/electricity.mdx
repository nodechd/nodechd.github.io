# Electricity

This section focuses on electricity. 

Units are:
- Voltage: volts (V)
- Current: amperes (A)
- Resistance: ohms (Ω)
- Power: watts (W), volts * amperes
- Energy: joules (J), volts * amperes * seconds

### Ohm's Law:
Voltage = Current * Resistance

### Power Law:
Power = Voltage * Current

### Energy Law:
Energy = Voltage * Current * Time = Power * Time

mAH is milliampere-hour, tells how many milliamperes a battery can provide for one hour. 
For example, a 2000 mAH battery can provide 2000 milliamperes for one hour, or 1000 milliamperes for two hours, etc.

With information on voltage and mAH, we can calculate the energy in joules a battery can provide. 
For example, a 3.7V 2000 mAH battery can provide 3.7 * 2 = 7.4 watt-hours of energy, or 7.4 * 3600 = 26640 joules of energy.

#### Discussion about 2 * 18650 Lithium ion battery which delivers 7.4V:

These are usually rated like 2 * 18650 3.7V 3300mAh rechargeable lithium ion batteries.
What this means is that the battery pack is made up of two 18650 lithium ion batteries connected in series, which gives 
a total voltage of 7.4V (3.7V + 3.7V). The capacity of the battery pack is still 3300mAh, which means it can provide 
3300 milliamperes for one hour, or 1650 milliamperes for two hours, etc.

Total Energy = Voltage * Capacity = 7.4V * 3.3Ah = 24.42 watt-hours of energy, or 
24.42 * 3600 = 87,912 joules of energy. 1 watt hour is equal to 3600 joules of energy.
